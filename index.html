The questions everyone asks

Straight answers.

So what actually is Gymbro?
One prompt. You paste it into an AI coding app once, it interviews you, and from then on it's your coach. Behind the scenes it writes your plan into a few plain files (your profile, your gym kit, your program, your logbook) and reads them back at the start of every session, so it always knows exactly where you left off. Nothing to install beyond the AI you already use.
Is it actually good, or just a gimmick?
It's built to a real standard: progressive overload, sensible volume, proper warm-ups, deloads, and hard safety rules. It won't hand you "1000 push-ups", won't program through an injury, and won't help you crash-diet. The aim is a plan a qualified trainer would look at and nod. It won't replace a great coach standing next to you fixing your form, but for planning, logging, and getting stronger over months, it genuinely holds up.
How does it remember my progress?
Every set you log gets written to your own logbook. Next session it reads your last numbers and sets your targets from there. Grind and miss reps, it backs off. Breeze through them, it adds weight. Months in, you can ask things like "how's my bench trending?" or "did I just hit a PR?" and it answers from your real history.
Which AI tools work, and do I need to pay?
Yes, a paid plan, but a normal tier is plenty for a few sessions a week: Claude Pro/Max for Claude Code, or ChatGPT Plus/Pro for Codex (Codex is built right into the ChatGPT app). GitHub is free. So there's no trainer bill, just the AI you probably already pay for. Plain ChatGPT without Codex won't fully work, and a newer, smarter model gives noticeably better coaching.
Can I really run it from my phone?
Yep. Open the Claude Code app (or the ChatGPT app and pick Codex), open your Gymbro folder, and tell it you're training. Because your data lives in that GitHub folder, your phone and laptop always see the same thing. It also speaks whatever language you write in.
Is my data private?
Yes, completely. Gymbro is just a prompt: it runs in your own AI account and your data lives in your own private repo, so I never see it, touch it, or store it. Nothing is shared or posted anywhere, and because it's open source you can read exactly what the prompt does before you run it.
